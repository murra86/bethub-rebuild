Synthesis — Multi-agent governance review of v3 data-layer and accounting-layer design
1. Coherence-of-framing — the four-agent picture
The four agents broadly converge on a single verdict: the suite coheres at the architectural level, but coheres around the wrong centre of gravity in one specific way that is load-bearing. None of them concluded "this doesn't even cohere." All four engaged with the proposal on its stated terms. But three of the four — the software developer, the project manager, and the open-questions agent — independently identified the same structural strain, and the skeptic acknowledged it as the sharpest finding in their own read.
The strain has one shape across all four outputs: the suite treats capture.db as a single data layer with a tunable cadence dial, when in fact the design has two different consumers with incompatible requirements layered on top of it — analytical (post-hoc, minute-tolerant, retention-indefinite) and operational (sub-second, in-the-moment, retention-zero). The data-layer document surfaces this distinction in §3 and §5.4 as a Session 19 operator-discovery, but the requirements doc and DUR were drafted before the distinction landed and have not been fully reconciled to it. The skeptic names this directly: "the analytical-versus-operational data distinction feels like a late realisation that quietly strains the periodic-only API and bracketing arguments." The project manager frames it as "you've identified it but not solved it." The software developer frames it as the core contradiction: "no duplication + no caching vs mission-critical read dependency — you can't have both." The open-questions agent frames it as the "Identity Contradiction" — building a lean operational tool while claiming it's a broad future-proof data harvester.
This is genuine framing strain, not surface patchwork. It is the load-bearing place where the four-agent picture says the suite does not yet cohere internally. Everything else the assessors found is either downstream of this strain (the periodic-only pattern straining, the bet schema question being harder than the binary framing suggests, the reachability question being underweighted) or independent of it (sequencing soundness, scope rightness, AccountCare trajectory).
The operator's secondary ask — "does this hang together as a coherent design or does session-by-session evolution show through as patchwork" — gets a nuanced answer. The skeptic's read is the most direct: the patchwork visible on careful reading is "honest artifacts of iterative evolution rather than sleight-of-hand," and the documents themselves surface their own risks. The other three assessors did not engage with the patchwork question explicitly but their substantive findings are consistent with the skeptic's read: the strain they identified is not a hidden incoherence the documents conceal — it is one the documents partially surface (data_layer_current §3 and §5.4) but have not yet propagated back through the other documents in the suite.
The synthesis-level finding: the suite coheres as a deliberate response to v2 failure modes, with one load-bearing strain that needs reconciliation before build. The strain is operationally visible (the analytical-versus-operational split) and the documents partially see it; the work outstanding is to follow through on what seeing it implies for the periodic-only pattern, the bet schema, and the integration boundary.
2. Question 1.1 — Bet schema simplification
The three assessors who engaged with this question split on the question, but in a more interesting pattern than a simple two-against-one. The skeptic lands cleanly on the leaner shape ("the leaner shape is the cleaner long-term commitment ... drop the inline fields and Slice 6 captures; resolve at read time"). The software developer lands cleanly on the richer shape, framing the leaner direction as "dangerous if taken too far" and warning that "bet records must be immutable facts" with at minimum price taken, available price context, and timestamped snapshot reference or copy. The project manager rejects the binary framing entirely: "Treating this as binary [is] the mistake ... You need a minimal immutable 'decision snapshot', not full denormalisation."
Where they agree: all three accept that the v2 schema mess was real and that some simplification is warranted. None of them defend the full v2 schema shape. All three name the same load-bearing dependency — both shapes lean on capture.db rigour — and all three accept that the question of which shape is right depends partly on how that rigour is established.
Where they disagree, and on what grounds: the skeptic weights the v2 lesson about dual sources of truth heavily (drift, dual-maintenance, schema-evolution pain) and argues the leaner shape is structurally cleaner because it forces single-source-of-truth discipline. The software developer weights a different v2-style risk: that some data is "volatile + external + not guaranteed reproducible" and must be captured at decision time or lost forever. The software developer's specific concern — that "historical bets change meaning retroactively" if capture.db is corrected later or has a backfill issue — is a substantive technical point the skeptic does not engage with. The skeptic dismisses the software developer's concern by leaning on the integration-boundary discipline ("schema drift surfaces in one place via vps_client") but that is a different argument than the one the software developer is making, which is about the immutability of the bet record as a historical fact rather than the cleanliness of the integration surface.
The project manager's middle position — minimal immutable decision snapshot, not full denormalisation — is the strongest reading of the three. It captures what the software developer is actually worried about (preserving the bet's decision context as an immutable fact) without committing to the v2-shaped richness the skeptic correctly identifies as the failure mode. It defines "minimal" by what answers the question "what did I see and act on?" — which is a different and tighter question than DR-026 currently answers.
Synthesis-derived observation: the binary framing in DUR Section 5 (entry 4) — "leaner-bet-record-with-periodic-only-API, or more-data-rich-bet-record-with-richer-API" — is what produces the disagreement. Once the question is reframed as "what is the minimal set of fields that must be captured at bet placement to make the bet record immutable as a decision-context fact, given that capture.db is the source of truth for everything else?" the three positions converge much more closely. The software developer would accept a tightly-scoped minimal snapshot (price taken, best available prices, liquidity snapshot, timestamp). The skeptic would accept it because it is not denormalisation of race-context. The project manager states it directly. The operator's instruction to weigh 1.1 and 1.5 together (§6) is consistent with this reframing.
3. Question 1.2 — Data-layer-first sequencing soundness
The skeptic and the project manager land on opposite verdicts on this question, and the disagreement is sharper and more substantive than on 1.1. The skeptic calls it "sound structural protection and one of the strongest parts of the proposal" — directly mitigating the build-now-discover-missing-fields-later risk that produces reversal cost. The project manager calls it the biggest delivery risk: "you've front-loaded uncertainty ... you don't yet know how you'll actually use the data in execution, but you're trying to finalise the data layer before seeing it in execution." The software developer did not engage with sequencing as a named question but their findings are largely compatible with the project manager's read (the design is "thoughtful but slightly idealised" and reliability needs to come first, which has sequencing implications).
The disagreement is real and turns on a substantive question: is the dominant risk for a solo-operator rebuild the cost of building against missing data fields, or the cost of locking a data contract before workflows have been validated? The skeptic and the project manager are looking at different reversal costs. The skeptic looks at v2's data problems and says: building first means assumptions about data availability that have proven costly. The project manager looks at v2's UX problems and says: locking the data layer first means assumptions about workflow usage that have not yet been pressure-tested, which produces awkward workarounds — "hello v2 again."
Both readings have force. The skeptic's read is correct that v2's data problems were baked in by tight coupling between execution and data, and that the operator's reversal-cost concern in the DUR is real. The project manager's read is correct that locking-without-using is a different kind of false certainty, and that the operator's three named concerns are workflow concerns that only surface in execution.
The strongest version of each side: the skeptic is arguing for sequencing as protection against the v2 failure mode that has actually happened. The project manager is arguing for sequencing that protects against a different v2 failure mode that has also happened — the one where the tool was built around assumptions about how it would be used and those assumptions turned out wrong. Neither is wrong on the merits.
Synthesis-derived observation: the disagreement is not actually about whether to do the data review before build, but about what counts as "locked." The project manager's "build thin v3 execution loop early, validate real workflows, THEN refine data layer" is not incompatible with the skeptic's "data review as pre-build gate" if the data review's output is a versioned and documented contract rather than a frozen schema — the contract can be extended (backward-compatibly) as workflow validation surfaces new requirements. DR-029 already describes versioned contracts with backward-compatible additions discipline; what the project manager's read adds is that the contract should be sized to support thin execution-loop work early, not finalised in a way that requires breaking changes when workflows reveal new needs. The data-layer-first sequencing is sound if "first" means "contract versioned and stable" rather than "contract feature-complete." The current documents do not draw this distinction sharply.
4. Question 1.3 — Data review scope rightness
The skeptic is the only assessor who engaged substantively with this question as a named scope question. Their read: "mostly right but conservatively drawn in ways that warrant minor expansion or explicit handling." In-scope items are appropriate. Out-of-scope decisions (full analytics layer, account-isolation formalisation, Cloudflare-blocked books) are reasonable. Three items they flagged for explicit attention: the operational-vs-analytical distinction needs resolution or explicit parking before/during the data review; empirical validation of capture.db population reliability and scraper health under load must be central; line-extraction/centre-line quality for sports must be a named sub-concern.
The project manager's read of scope is broader and more critical: "you're trying to future-proof too early ... optimising for future analysis over present usability." They argue for narrowing scope to "what supports decision-making + reconciliation now" and cutting "speculative analytics fields." This is a substantive disagreement with both the skeptic and the operator's stated direction in DUR Section 1 ("I want the data layer to capture what will be needed for analytics I haven't yet conceived of").
The software developer did not engage with scope as scope but their findings imply scope additions: monitoring/alerting/auto-restart infrastructure, separation of operational pricing from analytical capture, and explicit treatment of capture.db as unreliable input — all of which expand the scope rather than narrow it.
The open-questions agent identified one specific scope gap that the skeptic touched but did not foreground: the sports line-matching problem is an "architectural iceberg" — if capture.db cannot accurately centre a handicap line, the sports-betting side of v3 is fundamentally broken on launch, and this cannot be fixed later if schema and API are locked.
Where the four converge: the sports line-matching problem belongs in scope as a named sub-concern, not as a v2 carryover the architecture will somehow handle. The skeptic and open-questions agent name it directly; the software developer and project manager imply it through their concerns about operational fitness.
Where they diverge: the project manager is the lone voice for narrowing scope. The skeptic, software developer, and open-questions agent all push toward expansion (with the open-questions agent and software developer pushing toward expansion in specific directions — sports line-matching, monitoring/alerting/operational pricing — that the operator's named scope does not currently include).
Synthesis-derived observation: the future-proofing-vs-present-usability tension the project manager identifies is real, but the assessors' findings collectively suggest the right resolution is different from either "narrow speculatively" or "broaden speculatively." The expansion items the assessors named — sports line-matching, operational pricing distinction, continuous-fitness infrastructure, empirical population-state verification — are not future-analytics items; they are present-usability items the current scope underweights. The literature-derived analytics fields the project manager wants to cut are a different category. The synthesis position: cut speculative analytics field-capture from the data review's expansion ambitions (defer to when workflows actually need them), and add the operational/reliability items the other three assessors named. This is closer to the project manager's read on the analytics side and closer to the other assessors' read on the operational side.
5. Question 1.5 — Periodic-only API pattern with analytical bracketing
This is the question where the four-agent picture converges most clearly. All four assessors agree the periodic-only pattern is sound for analytical purposes and breaks down for operational purposes. None of them defends periodic-only as sufficient for execution-time decision support in the burst window.
The skeptic's framing is the cleanest: "Keep periodic-only for the vps_client contract serving race metadata, results, historical curves, and analytical bracketing; do not force it to serve live operational needs where a better tool (Betfair Streaming for exchange data) exists." The software developer agrees but adds a sharper point about the limit of cadence tuning: "volatility ≠ time-based ... markets jump in bursts, not smoothly," meaning that "cadence tuning solves this" is itself an undefended assumption. The project manager frames it as solving the wrong problem: "the real question [is] what did I see when I placed the bet, not what did the market look like around it." The open-questions agent frames it as a latency question the operator has not asked: "is a 60s-stale view compatible with 'frictionless execution'?"
The collective verdict: the periodic-only pattern is correct in scope but the scope as currently drawn is wrong. It is being asked to do double duty (analytical + operational) and the analytical justification (bracketing) does not transfer to operational use. This is the same finding as the analytical-versus-operational strain in §1 surfacing on the specific question.
Where they diverge: the skeptic and software developer both engage with the bracketing argument on its own terms and find it sound for analysis. The project manager sidesteps the bracketing argument and reframes the question — "what did I see" is a different question than "what did the market do around me," and the project manager's read is that the operator actually wants the former. The open-questions agent frames it similarly. This is a substantive disagreement about what the bet record's market-context fields are for. The skeptic's bracketing argument treats them as analytical inputs (where bracketing wins); the project manager's read treats them as decision-record fields (where the at-placement snapshot wins). This connects directly to the §2 disagreement on bet schema simplification.
Synthesis-derived observation: the bracketing-vs-at-placement question is structurally the same question as the bet-schema question, viewed from the API contract side instead of the storage side. Once you accept that the bet record needs to capture what the operator saw at placement (per the project manager and open-questions agent reading), some form of at-placement capture is needed — whether stored on the bet row (DR-026 inline) or fetched fresh at placement time via a non-periodic path. The periodic-only pattern is right for everything else (post-hoc bracketing, settlement, analytical curves). The pattern needs an explicit operational complement, not an extension.
6. Paired weighting — Questions 1.1 and 1.5 as one structural commitment
The operator asked the three assessors to weigh 1.1 and 1.5 together as one simpler-vs-more-complex choice. The three assessors' pairing-level outputs land in three different places.
The skeptic explicitly addresses the pairing and lands cleanly on the leaner shape: "the leaner shape (simpler bet record with full resolution from capture.db + periodic-only API for race context) is the right structural commitment. Both elements lean on capture.db rigour in the same place, eliminating duplication and dual-maintenance surfaces that produced v2's schema mess." The software developer addresses the pairing implicitly by rejecting both extremes: relax DR-028 slightly to allow targeted denormalisation on the bet record (price taken, best prices, liquidity, timestamp), keep periodic API but with volatility-aware staleness flags, and add a separate live-pricing layer for operational needs. The project manager addresses it by rejecting the binary framing of the pair as a whole: the operator is treating the pair as one simpler-vs-more-complex choice, but the actual structural commitment runs along a different axis — analytical vs operational — that cuts across both questions.
Where they converge: all three reject the maximalist richer-bet-record-plus-richer-API position. None of them defend the v2-shaped data-rich schema with cross-system durability framing as the right answer. The operator's current direction (DR-026 inline snapshot + Slice 6 field sizes + periodic-only API) does not have a defender among the three assessors.
Where they diverge: the skeptic defends the pure leaner position (no inline snapshot, no field-size captures, periodic-only) on grounds of architectural cleanliness and integration discipline. The software developer and project manager both argue for a middle position, but for slightly different reasons: the software developer because some context is "irreversible" and must be captured at decision time, the project manager because the right axis is operational vs analytical and the right design splits accordingly.
Synthesis-derived finding (which no single agent stated alone): the operator's framing of the paired choice — leaner-and-simpler vs more-data-rich-and-complex — is the wrong axis. The three assessors collectively suggest the right axis is operational-context-at-decision-time vs analytical-context-resolved-from-capture.db, and the right design captures the former on the bet record (small, immutable, decision-context only) and resolves the latter at read time via the periodic API. This is closer to the skeptic's leaner shape than to the operator's current direction, but it is not the skeptic's full leaner shape — it preserves a tightly-scoped at-placement snapshot that the skeptic argues against on dual-source-of-truth grounds.
The synthesis position: the bet record carries an immutable decision-context snapshot (price taken, best back/lay at placement, total matched, snapshot timestamp from operational source — not from periodic capture), and resolves everything else (race classification, runner detail, finish position, market curve for analysis, BSP) at read time via the periodic API. This is a different shape than either of the two the operator currently treats as the binary choice. It depends on the operational pricing layer existing (§8), which is itself the core synthesis-derived recommendation.
7. Question 2.1 — Reachability and continuous-fitness discipline
All four agents converge on this finding sharply, with no meaningful disagreement. The reachability and continuous-fitness gap is the load-bearing operational risk that the documents partially surface but do not yet address.
The empirical fact at the centre: v2 has been operating normally for at least six continuous days with the SSH tunnel down, with no operational impact, because v2's actual settlement path goes direct to Betfair API. v3 changes this — every bet log, every settlement, every burst review will exercise the integration. The documents themselves name this in data_layer_current §3 and §8 Question 1. The four agents collectively say: this is not adequately addressed by the bf_snapshot_unavailable graceful-degrade flag and a one-off pre-build review.
The skeptic: "the design needs explicit pre-build specification for monitoring, auto-restart of the tunnel/service, alerting/escalation, and lightweight ongoing fitness checks ... without this, the graceful degrade becomes the common path rather than the exception." The software developer: "you've already proven tunnel down for 6+ days, no monitoring, no alerting, no operational discipline. Now v3 depends on it for: every bet, every settlement, every review ... you'll get partial data, inconsistent behaviour, hard-to-detect drift." The project manager: "this is the sleeper issue that will kill you early ... directly hits your core goal: frictionless execution." The open-questions agent: "the design treats the VPS as a 'black box' that simply works ... if the VPS is the single source of truth, its failure doesn't just lose analytics data; it breaks the ability to settle and reconcile current operations."
This is the cleanest convergence in the four-agent review. The verdict: the reachability and continuous-fitness discipline must be specified pre-build, treated as a first-class requirement alongside the data review, not deferred. The graceful-degrade flag is necessary but insufficient. Specifically named items: tunnel monitoring, auto-restart, alerting, escalation thresholds, ongoing data-fitness checks (not just a one-off DR-029 verification), and explicit handling of silent degradation modes (which the software developer correctly identifies as worse than hard failures).
The synthesis is plain: this is the operator's "Concern 1" (reduce administrative overhead and detection time) reasserting itself at a different layer of the system. v3 currently does not solve Concern 1 in the integration layer because the integration layer does not have detection infrastructure built into it. Without this, the burst-review triage workflow that is supposed to surface anomalies is downstream of an integration that itself will silently degrade in ways the triage cannot reliably catch.
8. Question 2.2 — Operational live pricing (Betfair and soft-book)
8a. Operational Betfair live pricing
All three assessors who engaged with this question converge: specify a direct connection to the Betfair Streaming API for sub-second market data in the burst window. This is uncontroversial. The pattern exists, it is designed for this purpose, and it sits cleanly as an operational feed separate from the analytical capture.db path.
The skeptic frames the integration cleanly: route through a dedicated module (e.g. betfair_client) — extending the spirit of DR-028's one-integration-point discipline to operational feeds without forcing them through vps_client. Use the live stream when available, fall back to periodic capture.db snapshot. The software developer's "live pricing layer (new)" recommendation is the same shape. The project manager's "split data into two systems explicitly: operational layer real-time (Betfair streaming, etc.) and analytical layer (capture.db unchanged)" is the same shape.
There is no disagreement on 2.2a. The verdict: this is the right move, the operator should specify it before build, and it interacts cleanly with DR-026/027/028 once the operational/analytical distinction is propagated through the documents.
8b. Operational soft-book live pricing
Only the skeptic engaged with the four-option framework (A/B/C/D) at depth. Their read: a hybrid leaning on Option D (third-party odds-feed vendor) if a suitable one exists covering the day-one book list at acceptable cost, or Option B (last-known from capture.db with prominent staleness indicator) as conservative default. They explicitly point to The Odds API as a candidate covering Australian books (Sportsbet, TAB, Neds, Ladbrokes, Unibet, Betfair) and recommend vendor evaluation before build. They argue against Option A (full custom high-cadence scraping) on grounds of ongoing maintenance burden and adversarial surface area for a solo operator, and against Option C (on-demand per-review) on grounds that it adds burst-time latency and is still adversarial.
The software developer endorses adding an operational layer in principle but does not engage with the soft-book-specific complications. The project manager calls for an operational layer for "real-time (Betfair streaming, etc.)" but does not address the soft-book asymmetry. The open-questions agent does not engage with the four options.
Where the engagement converges: the soft-book operational case is harder than the Betfair case, and it must be resolved (or explicitly parked with a chosen direction) before the burst UI is designed, because the burst UI's decision-support story depends on what data freshness it can rely on.
Synthesis-derived observation: the skeptic's recommendation to scan vendor coverage as operator pre-decision-homework is the only concrete pre-build action proposed across the four agents. It is high-leverage: a vendor scan that finds 100% coverage of v3's day-one book list at acceptable cost shifts the question from "is operational soft-book viable" to "build vs buy with known cost on both sides," which is a much smaller decision. A scan that finds <80% coverage means Option B becomes the realistic default and the burst UI must be designed around explicit staleness rather than around live soft-book prices. The operator should run the scan before locking burst UI design, regardless of which option is ultimately chosen.
9. Question 2.3 — AccountCare-DB future-shape pushback
The skeptic is the only assessor who engaged substantively with this question. Their read: the operator's premise is reasonable and should stand. AccountCare is tightly coupled to bet events, promo lifecycle, free-bet ledger, and cash-flow events; it is not an independent high-volume or high-complexity domain that warrants separate ownership early. The trajectory toward a separate DB is "possible but not highly probable in the medium term for a solo operation."
The software developer pushes back on this premise. Their read: AccountCare affects "decision-making, restrictions, behavioural state over time" and will become "high-write, stateful, cross-cutting" — a separate bounded context, not just extra fields. They argue the integration discipline needs to scale now, not later, because the future state is plausibly multi-boundary (v3 ↔ capture.db, v3 ↔ accountcare, maybe more).
This is a substantive disagreement. The skeptic and software developer are weighing different things: the skeptic weights the current shape of AccountCare (warnings, tiers, phase tracking — coupled to existing v3 events) and concludes the trajectory is uncertain. The software developer weights what AccountCare could become if it grows into the role the operator might eventually want it to play (behavioural modelling, conditioning state over time) and concludes the trajectory is more probable than the operator's framing treats it.
The project manager and open-questions agent did not engage with this question.
Synthesis-derived observation: the disagreement turns on whether AccountCare's eventual role is closer to its current shape (a tightly-coupled extension of bet/promo events) or closer to a behavioural modelling layer. The skeptic is right on the current shape; the software developer is right that the trajectory is more probable than the operator's framing acknowledges if AccountCare grows into the behavioural-modelling role. The operator's preferred working hypothesis ("the answer surfaces from operating") is defensible for the current shape but underweights the probability that operating itself will push AccountCare toward the heavier role. The integration boundary discipline is currently designed for one boundary (v3 ↔ capture.db); the synthesis position is that designing it rigorously for one boundary in a way that replicates cleanly to a second boundary is the right hedge, which is consistent with both the skeptic's and the software developer's reads. DR-028's forbidden patterns and the single-module discipline already point in this direction; what would need to be added is explicit thinking about how the pattern replicates rather than how it scales — i.e., the next boundary gets its own module and contract, not a generalised cross-DB framework.
The four-agent picture is not as sharp here as elsewhere. The operator's framing is defensible on current grounds but the software developer's pushback should not be dismissed: it identifies a real probability the operator is underweighting.
10. Open-questions agent — what the named list did not reach
The open-questions agent's findings sharpen the per-question syntheses in three places and surface two concerns that land independently.
Where they sharpen: the analytical/operational strain (their "Identity Contradiction") converges with the §1 coherence-of-framing finding and the §5 periodic-only synthesis — they frame it as the operator wanting two different things from the same architecture. The reachability assumption ("VPS Rigor as a constant, not a variable") converges with §7 — they identify it as one of the three undefended load-bearing assumptions. The latency-of-decision-ready-data question converges with §5 and §8 — they frame it as a question the operator has not asked: "is a 60s-stale view compatible with frictionless execution?"
Where they sharpen specifically: the sports line-matching observation in their "Backgrounded items" is sharper than what any other agent produced. They name it as an architectural iceberg — if capture.db cannot accurately centre a handicap line, the sports-betting side is fundamentally broken on launch and cannot be fixed if schema and API are locked. The skeptic touched this in §4 as a sub-concern; the open-questions agent frames it as load-bearing. This deserves elevation in the data review scope.
Where they land independently:
The operator-as-error-free-transcriber assumption (their "Assumption B"). The design assumes manual book-settlement entry is the reliable ground truth for cash flow, but v2's failure mode was operator-introduced errors. v3's reconciliation surfaces flag divergences but cannot help the operator determine whether the VPS is wrong or they are wrong. This is a real undefended assumption that no other assessor named. It interacts with the burst-review triage workflow — if the operator is the canonical source for one side of every cash-flow reconciliation and is also error-prone, the triage workflow needs explicit support for "which side is wrong" investigation, not just "these diverge."
The Claude-driven triage as load-bearing pillar (their "Backgrounded items" observation). The documents treat the burst-review triage as a workflow preference; the open-questions agent argues it is the only mechanism keeping v3 from drowning in the same administrative debt as v2. If the Claude integration becomes cumbersome, the six reconciliation surfaces become a wall of noise the operator will eventually ignore — exactly like v2's flagged-items page. This is a sharp finding the other assessors did not produce. It deserves engagement: the burst-review workflow is currently described as a capability rather than as a load-bearing design pillar.
The write-side validation question (their "Question 1"). The operator asks about schema shape but not about validation at the moment of bet entry. If the schema is simplified to IDs and timestamps, a typo in a runner ID or market ID at entry creates a permanent ghost record that no triage can recover, because the link to capture.db is broken at creation. This is a substantive concern that combines with §2 — the leaner the bet schema, the higher the cost of an invalid identifier at bet placement, which means write-side validation discipline (does the runner_id resolve in capture.db? does the market_id exist? is the placement timestamp inside the race's pre-jump window?) becomes more important under the leaner shape than under the richer shape.
11. Synthesis-derived recommendations
The four agents collectively produced findings that no single agent stated alone. The recommendations below are labelled as synthesis-derived and trace which agents' findings they draw on. Some of these are genuine triangulation; others are reframings of single-agent findings that the cross-agent picture sharpens.
Recommendation 1: Reframe the bet-schema and periodic-API pair on the analytical/operational axis, not the simpler/richer axis. The operator's binary framing in DUR Section 5 (entry 4) and B.7 #1+#5 — leaner-and-simpler vs more-data-rich-and-complex — is the wrong axis. The right axis is operational-decision-context (small, immutable, captured at bet placement from an operational source) vs analytical-context (resolved at read time from capture.db via the periodic API). The bet record carries an immutable at-placement snapshot for decision context only — price taken, best back/lay, total matched, timestamp. Everything else (race classification, runner detail, finish position, market curve, BSP, field size at settlement) is resolved at read time. Draws on: project manager's reframing of the binary as wrong, software developer's "irreversible context" finding, skeptic's single-source-of-truth concern, open-questions agent's "what did I see" framing.
Recommendation 2: Specify operational live pricing (Betfair Streaming + soft-book direction) as part of the pre-build data review, not as a deferred concern. The analytical/operational distinction surfaced in data_layer_current §3 and §5.4 has not propagated through the rest of the suite, and the periodic-only pattern strains because of it. The pre-build work should produce: a direct Betfair Streaming integration through a dedicated module (e.g. betfair_client) for execution-time race pricing; and a chosen direction for soft-book operational pricing (Option D vendor scan completed before lock, or Option B explicit staleness if vendor scan fails). Burst UI design depends on this; deferring it forces design against optimistic data-freshness assumptions. Draws on: software developer, project manager, skeptic all converging on the analytical/operational split; skeptic's specific Option D vendor-scan recommendation; open-questions agent's latency-of-decision-ready-data question.
Recommendation 3: Treat reachability and continuous-fitness as a first-class pre-build deliverable alongside the data review. All four agents converged on this. The deliverable should specify: tunnel monitoring with auto-restart, alerting with thresholds, ongoing data-fitness checks (not just one-off DR-029), explicit handling of silent degradation, and integration with the burst-review triage so that integration health is a first-class anomaly category alongside the six reconciliation surfaces. The graceful-degrade flag is necessary but insufficient. Draws on: all four agents converging.
Recommendation 4: Elevate sports line-matching and write-side validation to named scope items in the data review. The line-matching problem is currently a sub-concern in DUR Section 4 and a footnote in v3_data_requirements B.3. The open-questions agent frames it as architectural — if capture.db cannot expose a clean centre line, the sports side is broken on launch and cannot be retrofitted. The skeptic's read on scope agrees this should be a named sub-concern. Write-side validation (does a bet's identifiers resolve in capture.db at the moment of bet entry?) is implicit in the leaner schema's correctness story but not specified anywhere. The data review scope should include both as named items. Draws on: open-questions agent's iceberg framing for line-matching and write-side validation; skeptic's scope-rightness sub-concern.
Recommendation 5: Reframe Claude-driven burst-review triage from "capability" to "load-bearing design pillar." This is largely the open-questions agent's finding but it is consistent with the other three agents' implicit treatment. The triage workflow is the v3 successor to v2's flagged-items page, which failed because it was a passive surface.
