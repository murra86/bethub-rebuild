1 · The selection stack (replaces the person/book dropdowns)

Three taps arm everything, all targets visible at once — no opening a list to see what's in it. Book chips carry the live balance so "can I even bet here" is answered at a glance; a $0 book fades but stays tappable. Book order is set once at day-open (yesterday's most active first) and then frozen for the day — buttons that shuffle mid-session break muscle memory, which matters more at burst pace than perfect ordering.

The lay confirmation and the back log become one continuous box — no hunting for the LOG button afterwards (this morning's $50 free-bet miss). Person and book arrive preselected from the top bar (or the last back you logged), stake prefills from the free-bet face, odds from the soft price you typed — so the usual case is zero to two taps then Log. "Skip" keeps the never-blocked rule; a skipped back still shows as the board's ⚠ unpaired-lay flag and lands in Burst review.

The UX principles doing the work here (so you can push back on the right level): buttons beat dropdowns whenever there are seven or fewer options — everything is visible and one tap instead of two; most-likely-next is preselected so the common case is confirming, not choosing; frequency orders, position sticks — ordering by activity helps only if things stay where your thumb learned they are; and the key number rides the button (book balances) so decisions don't need a trip to another page.
